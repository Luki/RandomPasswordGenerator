Password security basics

How to create a strong password

A strong password is one of the easiest ways to protect your online accounts from unauthorized access.

A good password should be difficult for other people and automated programs to guess, while still being manageable for you.

Use a long password

Password length is one of the most important factors in password security. Aim for at least 12-16 characters, and use longer passwords whenever possible. The more characters a password contains, the harder it is to crack.

Mix different characters

Use a combination of:

  • Uppercase letters (A-Z)
  • Lowercase letters (a-z)
  • Numbers (0-9)
  • Special characters such as !, @, #, or $

For example, instead of using password123, create something much more difficult to guess, such as a randomly generated password containing a mixture of letters, numbers, and symbols.

Avoid common information

Never use information that can easily be associated with you. Avoid:

  • Your name or username
  • Birthday or age
  • Phone numbers
  • Names of family members or pets
  • Addresses
  • Favorite teams or celebrities
  • Common words such as password, qwerty, or 123456

Hackers can often find personal information online and use it to make targeted guesses.

Do not reuse passwords

Use a unique password for every important account. If one website suffers a data breach and your password is reused elsewhere, attackers may try the same password on your other accounts.

A password manager can help you create and securely store unique passwords without requiring you to memorize all of them.

Consider a passphrase

Another good option is a long, randomly generated passphrase made from multiple unrelated words. The important thing is that it should be long, unique, and difficult to predict.

Avoid common phrases, song lyrics, quotes, or sentences that someone could easily guess.

Use a password generator

If you do not want to create a password yourself, a random password generator can create strong passwords using randomly selected characters. This can help you avoid predictable patterns and common passwords.

Remember: A strong password should be long, unique, random, and difficult to guess.

For additional protection, enable two-factor authentication (2FA) whenever an account supports it. Even if someone discovers your password, 2FA can provide another layer of security.
